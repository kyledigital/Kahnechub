Content strategy · Kyle Hector

What’s in your content library?

Some brands have one tank, and it only comes out when there’s a sale.

A tank, a fighter jet and a supply truck do different jobs. I think about content the same way. A tutorial helps someone figure things out. A customer story gives them a picture of what it’s like to use the product. A funny post might be the thing they send round the group chat.

When I want to see which of those jobs a brand has been overlooking, I think of a library.

Check the shelves

Imagine walking into a library and finding six copies of the same book, with whole shelves sitting empty. A brand’s feed can feel like that. It might be quiet for weeks, or it might have three posts a week about the same offer. Either way, there’s only one reason to look through it.

The brand might have a brilliant answer to a question customers keep asking in the DMs. That answer could help the people who haven’t thought to ask yet. Put it on the shelf where they can find it.

A bakery has more to talk about than prices

Say you run a bakery. Film the kitchen at five in the morning while the first batch is going in. Show someone how to tell when their dough has risen enough. Ask the customer who comes in every Friday why they always order the same thing. Make a skit about the person who says they only want a taste, then eats half your slice.

Now you have things to post between offers. A lot of the material is already there in what you make, the questions people ask, and the things you laugh about at work. Keep a note of them as they happen. A good idea on Tuesday can save you staring at a blank calendar on Friday.

Give AI something worth working with

AI can help turn a voice note from the baker into a short script, or help you find a few ideas in a long interview. Listen to the recording yourself. The throwaway line about burning the first batch might be the bit people remember. You have to be there, or speak to someone who was, to catch details like that.

Look at your last ten posts

Next to each post, finish the sentence “This gives the reader…” If most of the answers are “a price” or “a deadline”, pick one of those empty shelves and give it a post this week. Start with the question you’ve already answered twice today.
